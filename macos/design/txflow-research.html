Transaction Flow Research

How the leading fields show what one transaction did, and what that means for the flow picture on the Transaction page. Each example is a transaction on the Verus chain, read from the local node.

The short answer: the flow picture today is a good drawing of a Bitcoin payment. About one transaction in ten on this chain is a payment of that kind. The fields that show complex transactions well do not start with a flow drawing. They start with a sentence, then a statement of who ended with more and who ended with less in each currency, and they keep the output-by-output record as a list. A picture built on that statement stays correct for each kind of transaction that Verus has.

What transactions are

  • Most transactions are not payments. On a quiet day, six of each ten transactions are block rewards and three are stakes. On a busy day, most of the others are protocol transactions.
  • A stake moves nothing. Its input and its output are the same amount at the same address. A flow picture shows the full amount in motion.
  • The reserve passes through. An import spends the reserve deposits of a currency and makes them again. In one example, 347,397 VRSC goes in and 347,328 VRSC comes out. The difference of less than 100 VRSC is what the transaction did.
  • Value is in more than one currency. In the busy range, 723 of 1,226 transactions carry a currency other than VRSC, 504 carry two or more, and one carries 26. Amounts of different currencies do not share a scale.
  • The purpose is frequently not value. A finalization, an import mark or a fee pool record carries no coins. A picture of coins leaves these out.
  • A small number are very wide. Most transactions have one or two inputs and two outputs. The widest in these ranges have 392 inputs or 740 outputs.
  • The chain has no input-to-output link. All inputs of a transaction go into one sum, and all outputs come out of it. A line from one input to one output would be an invention.
  • A hidden amount has a public total. The amount that enters or leaves the shielded pool is public. The recipients and the split are not.

What the leading fields do

Bitcoin explorers

The flow diagram of mempool.space is the origin of the picture today: inputs on the left, outputs on the right, one knot in the middle, widths in proportion to value. Its source code has rules for the hard cases. A line is never thinner than 2 points. More than 250 lines on a side are joined into one. The fee is a line of its own. An output with no value is a short stub. An unknown value takes an equal part of what is left. A click on a line end opens the transaction that made or spent it.

For Carbide Silica: the form is correct when the chain has one currency and most transactions are payments. The stub, the join and the navigation are worth keeping.

Explorers of contract chains

Etherscan and Solscan put one line of words first: the transaction action. Etherscan says that the line removes the miscellaneous data and keeps the events that matter to a person, and that many transactions do not have one. Solscan then gives one table of balance changes: each address, each token, the balance before and after.

For Carbide Silica: a sentence first, made only from facts. When no sentence applies, say the kind and stop.

Security explorers

Phalcon, Tenderly and EigenTx are made for transactions with tens of transfers. Each gives three views of one transaction: a graph of the transfers with an address as a node and a numbered transfer as a line, a table of the net change of each address, and the call record. Tenderly adds a filter for one token and a control that steps through the transfers in order. The lines do not have widths.

For Carbide Silica: the net change of each party is a view of its own, apart from the record of each output. A filter for one currency is the answer to many currencies.

Wallets

Before a person signs, MetaMask and Rabby show the estimated balance changes: what leaves the wallet and what arrives. The clear signing standard ERC-7730 has the same aim for hardware wallets: readable fields in place of raw data. The writers of the standard say that readable is not the same as verified.

For Carbide Silica: "who ended with more, who ended with less" is the form that most people read correctly. Each figure must come from the transaction, not from a description of it.

Investigation tools

Chainalysis Reactor draws entities, not addresses: a cluster of addresses is one node. It does not show where funds go after a deposit at a service, because the service pools the funds and the path would be wrong.

For Carbide Silica: group the outputs by party. Do not draw a path through the reserve deposits, which pool value in the same way.

Research on flow diagrams

Arunkumar and others tested alluvial diagrams with 100 people. People found the largest flow correctly 79 times in 100 and the largest group 57 times in 100, and each added group or flow made the result worse. Zhao and others found that a grid was better than a Sankey diagram when people had to read the value of a link. Guides for Sankey diagrams give the same limit: about ten nodes on a side, and small flows joined into one.

For Carbide Silica: few parties, exact figures in a grid, and one joined row for the small ones.

Research on change

Möser and Narayanan tested 26 rules that guess which output of a Bitcoin transaction is the change. Each rule finds 10 to 30 of each 100 change outputs. Verified data exists for about 5 of each 100 transactions.

For Carbide Silica: say "change" only when the output goes back to an address that paid. That is a fact. Each other guess is not shown.

Research on uncertainty

Boukhelifa and others compared four marks for a line that is not certain: blur, dashes, grey and a sketched line. People preferred dashes. The sketched line was as easy to understand as blur.

For Carbide Silica: a dashed outline for a hidden recipient. The public total keeps its true length.

The same transactions, drawn three ways

Select a transaction, then a form. The first form is the picture today, drawn by the rules of the app. The second form uses the rules of mempool.space. The third form is the proposal.

The first two forms draw VRSC only, as the picture today does. In the statement, a hollow bar to the left is an amount that a party gave. A solid bar to the right is an amount that a party received. Each currency has its own scale, and each column sums to zero.

The transfer graph is the form of Phalcon and Tenderly. It fits a chain where a transfer has one sender and one recipient. A Verus transaction does not have that link, thus the graph has no lines to draw between an input and an output.

Recommendation

Replace the flow picture with a statement by party, and keep the lists of inputs and outputs below it as the full record.

  1. One sentence first. The sentence comes from the tags, the totals and the output data that Carbide gives. It names the kind, the parties and the amounts. A reserve transfer says what was asked for: the amount, the currency it goes through and the currency it becomes.
  2. Group by party. All inputs and outputs of one address are one row. The reserve deposits of a currency are one party, and the waiting transfers are one party. Each row shows the net change: a hollow bar for an amount given, a solid bar for an amount received, and the exact figure.
  3. One column for each currency. Each column has its own scale, and its bars sum to zero. A row for the fee, or for inputs less outputs, closes the column. A control shows one currency at full width when there are more than five.
  4. Passed through is a figure. An amount that a party both gave and received is not a bar. It is one line below the statement: "347,328 VRSC of the reserve passed through."
  5. Outputs without value are marks. Imports, exports, notarizations, finalizations, evidence and the fee pool are named below the statement. Each mark opens the output or the page it refers to.
  6. Many parties become a spread. The statement shows the five largest and joins the others into one row. One strip below has a segment for each party, largest first, with the median in words.
  7. Hidden is dashed. The shielded pool is a row. Its bar has the length of the public total and a dashed outline. The text says that the recipient is hidden.
  8. Each row leads to the record. A click on a row marks its inputs and outputs in the lists below. The lists keep each output, its tags and its links, as they are now.

The flow diagram is not kept as a second view. For a payment it shows what the statement shows. For each other kind it is wrong or empty.

What stays as Carbide gives it

  • The fee. Carbide gives a fee only when the totals make one. For a protocol transaction it gives the difference between the public inputs and outputs, and the page uses that name.
  • The tags and annotations of each output. The marks and the sentence use them and do not add to them.
  • Hidden values. The page shows the totals that Carbide derives from the value balance. It does not estimate a recipient or a split.
  • Addresses. A row is an address or an identity, not a person. Two addresses of one owner are two rows, and the page does not join them.
  • Change. The word is used only for an amount that goes back to an address that paid.
  • The lists of inputs and outputs, the raw data and the links between protocol outputs.

Limits of this study

  • The measures are from two ranges: 1,500 recent blocks and 300 blocks with much DeFi activity. Other ranges can have other proportions.
  • The first two forms on this page follow the rules of the app and of mempool.space. They are drawings for comparison, not screen captures.
  • The documents of Phalcon and Solscan were not available to read in full. Their descriptions here are from the search results of their own pages.
  • No example in this study makes or destroys a currency in an import. When that occurs, a column does not sum to zero from the parties alone. The closing row then shows the difference with the name "not from inputs" or "not in outputs".
  • The sentences on this page were written for the eleven examples. The rules that make them for each transaction are part of the build.
