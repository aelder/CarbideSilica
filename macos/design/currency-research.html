Currency Research

How the leading fields show a pool of reserves and its prices through time, and what that means for the "Over time" lines and the batch picture on the Currency page. Each figure uses basket currencies on the Verus chain, read from the local node.

The short answer: the page gives the price of a basket in each of its reserves, each on its own scale. A line that goes up means that the basket costs more of that reserve, thus that the reserve lost value. A person reads it the other way. In one real period, the lines for VRSC and vETH went up while VRSC and vETH fell. The fields that show baskets and pools well let the person select one unit, show each price in that unit, and put the lines on one scale that starts at 100.

What a basket currency is

  • 31 of the 57 currencies on the chain are baskets. A basket holds 1 to 10 reserves. 21 of the 31 give each reserve the same weight.
  • The value of each reserve is fixed by its weight. In a basket, the amount of a reserve divided by its price is its weight times the supply. A chart of the parts by value is the same picture at each moment.
  • What moves is the rate between the reserves. A conversion puts one reserve in and takes a different one out. The pool is a market between its reserves, and the prices that it implies are the data.
  • "Price in reserve" is the price of the basket, not of the reserve. It is the number of units of the reserve for one unit of the basket. When a reserve loses value, the number goes up.
  • The pools are very different in size. The largest holds the value of 10.9 million VRSC. 19 of the 31 hold less than 10,000.
  • No pool changed in the last 30 days. The state of each of the 31 baskets at the tip is the same as 43,200 blocks before. The examples on this page are therefore from 48 days early in the year, when two pools changed in almost each sample.

What the page shows today

  • One line for each reserve, each on its own scale. The lines cannot be compared with each other, and the size of a change is not readable.
  • The direction is the reverse of what a person expects. The label is the name of the reserve and "price per unit". The line shows the basket's price in that reserve.
  • There is no common unit. The page does not say what one unit of the basket is worth in one currency that the person selects, or what the pool says that one VRSC is worth.
  • The reserves table shows amounts that cannot be added. 54,007 VRSC and 17.8 vETH are the same value in the pool. The table does not say so.
  • The batch picture has no common scale. Its lines have widths in the units of the pool, and the two sides of a conversion are different currencies.
  • The cursor and the exact state are good. One slider moves all the figures, and each point is a real block. These parts stay.

What the leading fields do

Foreign exchange

A cross rate table shows the price of each currency in one base currency, and the person can change the base. The rate between two currencies comes from their rates against the base. The convention is stated on the table: which currency is the unit.

For Carbide Silica: one control that selects the unit. Each price on the page is then "one of this is worth so much of the unit".

Pool pages of exchanges

The pool pages of Uniswap and of tools such as Tenderly show the value in the pool, the amounts of each token, and one price chart. A control turns the pair around, so that the person selects which token is the unit. They show depth as the trade size that moves the price by 1 in 100.

For Carbide Silica: the value in the pool in the selected unit, and one chart of prices in that unit.

Weighted pools

Balancer's documents state the rule of a weighted pool: when the pool agrees with the market, the weight of a token is its part of the value of the pool. The value of a balance needs a price, and the pool itself gives the prices between its tokens.

For Carbide Silica: say that each reserve holds its weight of the value, and show the amounts beside it.

Index charts

To compare series with different units, statistics offices and chart tools set each series to 100 at one date and plot all on one scale. The method goes back to Bertin. Guides give one warning: the chart compares changes, not levels, and must say so.

For Carbide Silica: one panel with each reserve and the basket as a line from 100 at the start of the range, in the selected unit.

Fund documents

A fund factsheet has the same parts for each fund: the base currency, the holdings with their weights, and one line of the value of the fund in the base currency. The factsheet states that the result is different in a different currency.

For Carbide Silica: the basket is a fund of its reserves. The same three parts, with the unit stated.

Batch settlement

The records of a batch auction show, for each token, the amount that came in and the amount that went out, at one price. The study of the transaction page reached the same form: a statement with a column for each currency.

For Carbide Silica: the last batch as one row for each reserve, with what came in and what went out, and the value of each in the selected unit.

Two real pools, drawn two ways

Select a pool and a form. In the proposal, select the unit. The time is blocks 3,900,000 to 3,969,600, about 48 days.

In the proposal, each line starts at 100. A line at 80 means that the currency lost 20 of each 100 of its value in the selected unit. The heavy line is the basket.

Recommendation

Add a unit control to the Currency page of a basket, and show each price, each value and each line in that unit. Keep the cursor, the exact state and the activity strip.

  1. One sentence first. "Bridge.vETH is a basket of 4 reserves at 25 of each 100. One Bridge.vETH is worth 14.76 VRSC. The pool holds the value of 239,569 VRSC."
  2. A unit control. The reserves of the basket are the choices. The chain's own coin is the first choice when it is a reserve. The page states the unit in each heading.
  3. The reserves table gets two columns. The value of each reserve in the unit, and the price of one of that reserve in the unit. The table says that each reserve holds its weight of the value.
  4. One panel of lines from 100. The basket and each reserve, in the unit, from 100 at the start of the range. Each line has its name and its change at its end. The supply stays as a second panel on the same time axis.
  5. The last batch as a statement. One row for each reserve that moved: the amount in or out, and its value in the unit. It uses the bars of the transaction statement.
  6. Say when nothing moved. "No conversion in this range" in place of flat lines, as the page does now for the supply.
  7. The terminal's figures stay in view. "Price in reserve" stays as a column, with one line that says what it is: units of the reserve for one unit of the basket.

What stays as Carbide gives it

  • The definition, the state, the weights, the reserves and "price in reserve", as the node gives them.
  • The state at a chosen block, and the imports and exports around it.
  • Each price in a unit is the pool's own rate between two of its reserves at that block. It is not a market quote, and the page says so.
  • A currency that is not a basket has no reserves, and its page does not change.

Limits of this study

  • The two examples are from one period of 48 days, sampled each 480 blocks.
  • A rate between two reserves is only as good as the trade in the pool. In a pool with no conversions, the rate is old.
  • The study did not measure depth: the size of a conversion that moves a rate by a set amount.
  • The pages of Uniswap and Balancer were read as documents and summaries, not in use.
  • The page does not test the forms with people.
