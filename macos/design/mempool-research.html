Mempool Research

How the leading fields show transactions that wait for a block, and what that means for the tiles on the Mempool page. Each figure uses the Verus chain, read from the local node.

The short answer: the tiles are a form for a full mempool where transactions compete for space. The Verus mempool is not that. It is empty most of the time, it holds one transaction when it is not empty, and each block takes all that waits. The fields that show a small queue well do not draw the size of each item. They show what waits, how long it has waited against the usual wait, when it will leave, and what left most recently.

What the mempool is

  • It is empty most of the time. On a quiet day, 1,365 of 1,500 blocks took nothing from the mempool. On a busy day, 106 of 300 took nothing.
  • When it is not empty, it holds a small number. On a quiet day, 94 of the 135 blocks that took a transaction took one. The largest number in the busy range is 36.
  • Nothing competes for space. The largest block in the two ranges is 72 KB. The limit is 2,000 KB. Each block has room for all that waits, thus a fee does not buy a place.
  • The wait is the wait for the next block. Half of the waits between blocks on the quiet day are shorter than 14 seconds, and 4 of each 100 are longer than 5 minutes. A transaction in the mempool is in that same wait.
  • The mempool fills during a long wait. On the quiet day, a block that came more than 2 minutes after the last one took 0.59 transactions on average. A block that came less than 10 seconds after took 0.06.
  • The kind is the content. On the quiet day the transactions are payments. On the busy day, 79 of each 100 are conversions, imports, exports and notarizations.

What the tiles show today

  • The usual picture is an empty box or one tile. One transaction fills the full area. During this study the page showed one blue rectangle with the word "Transaction".
  • The size of a tile is its bytes. Bytes decide nothing here, because no block is near full.
  • The shade is the age, to 90 seconds. The shade has no scale, and it does not show the age against the usual wait for a block.
  • The tile does not say what the transaction does. The label is the first tag, or "Transaction" for a payment.
  • The page does not show what occurs next or what occurred last. The time of the last block is in the line of totals. The transactions that left with it are not on the page.
  • The tiles move. They are in order of size, thus each arrival changes the position of the others.

The tiles of Carbide's terminal are not this form. They are cards in a list, one for each transaction, with the arrival, the fee, the entry height, the parents and the tags.

What the leading fields do

Bitcoin mempool explorers

mempool.space shows the mempool as projected blocks: the blocks that miners will make if they take the highest fee rates first. Its fee estimates are the median rates of the first three projected blocks. Johoe's charts show the mempool through time as stacked bands of fee rate. Both forms answer one question: what fee rate gets a transaction into a block.

For Carbide Silica: that question does not exist on Verus now. The useful part is the projected block: one block, with the part of its space that the waiting transactions fill.

Live transaction displays

TxStreet draws each transaction as a person who walks to a bus, and the bus leaves when a block is made. The size of the person is the size of the transaction. Bitfeed shows the same path from unconfirmed to confirmed. The metaphor is a waiting room: arrival, wait, departure.

For Carbide Silica: show the arrival, the wait and the departure of each transaction. The size is the least useful part of these displays.

Research on data streams

Huron, Vuillemot and Fekete made visual sedimentation for items that arrive at irregular rates. A new item is shown alone. As it gets old it joins a layer that shows only the total. New data stays readable, and old data stays in view without clutter.

For Carbide Silica: a waiting transaction gets a full row. A transaction that left is one mark on its block.

Transaction monitors

Blocknative reports each change of state of a transaction: pending, stuck, dropped, confirmed. The record of a transaction is a sequence of states, not one position in a picture.

For Carbide Silica: keep a transaction on the page after it leaves the mempool, with the block that took it or the fact that the node dropped it.

Passenger information

A departure board lists what waits and when it leaves. Studies of displays at stops in London, The Hague and Seattle found that people with wait information judged their wait to be 13 to 28 of each 100 shorter.

For Carbide Silica: the time of the next block is not known, but the record of recent waits is. Show the wait so far against that record, as the chain pulse does.

Queue analysis

Traffic engineers draw a queue as two cumulative lines on one time axis: arrivals and departures. The vertical distance between the lines is the number that waits. The horizontal distance is the wait of one item.

For Carbide Silica: one time axis with the blocks on it, and one line for each transaction from its arrival to its block.

Research on treemaps

Kong, Heer and Agrawala tested how people compare the areas of treemap tiles. People compare areas with less accuracy than lengths, and the form is useful where a bar chart has no room: thousands of items. Nielsen Norman Group gives the same limit: a treemap does not permit exact comparison.

For Carbide Silica: one to thirty-six items is not a quantity for a treemap. A list has room for each one.

Empty states

The design systems of IBM and SAP give one rule for a view with no data: say why it is empty and what will change that. For data that comes with time, say what the view waits for.

For Carbide Silica: the empty page is the usual page. It must carry information: the last block, what it took, and the recent record.

A record of the live mempool

Real moments, drawn two ways

Each moment is the mempool immediately before a real block: the transactions that the block took. Select a moment, then a form.

The arrival time of each transaction in these blocks is not in the chain, thus the tiles have one shade and the list has no wait for each row. The live page has both.

Recommendation

Replace the tiles with a waiting list on a time record. Keep the sortable list as the second view.

  1. One sentence first. "Nothing waits. The last block came 40 seconds ago and took 3 transactions." Or: "2 transactions wait. The last block came 3 minutes ago, longer than 89 of 100 recent waits."
  2. A time record with the blocks on it. The last 15 minutes on one axis that ends at the present. Each block is a mark, as in the chain pulse. Each transaction that the app saw is a line from its arrival to the block that took it. A line that is open at the right is a transaction that waits now.
  3. A row for each waiting transaction. The row has the kind, the sentence of the transaction statement, the wait, the fee, the size, the expiry in blocks, and the parents that also wait. The rows are in order of arrival and do not move. A child is below its parent.
  4. The next block as one bar. The bytes that wait as a part of the 2,000 KB limit, with the words "all of these fit in the next block". If the queue is ever larger than one block, the bar shows that, and the fee rate then matters.
  5. What left. Below the waiting rows, the transactions that the last blocks took, with the block and the wait of each one that the app saw. A person who sent a transaction finds it here after it leaves the mempool.
  6. Many transactions get counts first. Above 12 rows, the page shows the number of each kind, and each count is a filter.
  7. The list stays. The second view is the table of the terminal: arrival, fee, size, entry height, parents and tags, with a sort on each column.

What stays as Carbide gives it

  • The mempool comes only from a node, each 2 seconds, and is not stored. An old mempool says nothing about the mempool now.
  • The tags of each transaction, and "classification pending" until the app has the transaction.
  • The fee, the size, the entry height and the parents that the node gives.
  • The totals: the number of transactions, the serialized size, the last block and the refresh state.
  • The app does not predict the time of the next block. It shows the record of recent waits.

Limits of this study

  • The chain does not record when a transaction arrived. The counts for each block are what the block took, which is what waited at that moment or was made with the block.
  • Imports and exports can be made by the node that makes the block. Some of them do not wait in the mempool of other nodes.
  • The live record is short. The node was quiet during the study.
  • The two ranges are 1,500 recent blocks and 300 blocks with much DeFi activity. Other ranges can have other proportions.
  • The source pages of Bitfeed and of the sedimentation paper were not available to read in full. Their descriptions here are from the summaries of their own pages.
  • The page does not test the forms with people.
