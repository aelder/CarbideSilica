Identity Research

How the leading fields show who controls an account and how that control changed, and what that means for the control diagram and the version line on the Identity page. Each figure uses identities on the Verus chain, read from the local node.

The short answer: the page today answers "who holds each power" with a drawing of boxes and lines, and it shows the history as a row of dots and a long list. The drawing has three fixed parts for each identity, thus it is a table in the form of a diagram. It leaves out the lock, which half of the identities in this study use, and which is what most updates change. The fields that show control well give a table of powers, the outcome of each failure in words, and one history with a lane for each power.

What an identity is

  • One key is the rule. 68 of the 69 identities have one primary address. One has two addresses and needs one signature.
  • There are three patterns of control. 30 identities have one identity that can revoke and a different one that can recover. 22 have one identity for the two powers. 16 are their own authority, and a lost or stolen key has no remedy.
  • Half use the lock. 33 of 69 have a lock now. With a lock, funds cannot move until an unlock is requested and a set number of blocks pass. The delays in the sample are from 480 blocks, about 8 hours, to 525,600 blocks, about one year.
  • Most updates are the lock. 241 of 334 updates changed the lock and nothing else of control. The keys changed in 41, and an authority in fewer than 40.
  • Revocation is used. 12 of the 69 were revoked at some time and are active again.
  • Histories are short, with exceptions. The median identity has 3 versions. Six have more than 20, and the longest has 33.

What the page shows today

  • The control drawing is three boxes for each identity. A key on the left, the identity in the middle, and the two authorities on the right. The layout is the same each time, thus the lines carry no data.
  • The outcomes are behind tabs. "Stolen keys" and "Recovery" are two more versions of the drawing. A person sees one case at a time and must remember the others.
  • The lock is not in the drawing. It is one line in the list of fields below. For a locked identity, the lock decides if a thief can move funds before the owner acts.
  • The version line marks lock changes as "other data". A blue mark is a change of who controls the identity, and a grey mark is all other changes. Seven of each ten marks in the sample are lock changes, and they are grey.
  • The history of the authorities is a list with two rows for each version. One identity with 21 versions and no change of its authorities gets 42 rows that say the same thing.
  • The page does not say who controls the authorities. If the same key controls the identity and its recovery identity, the recovery adds nothing.

What the leading fields do

Shared wallets

Safe shows the control of an account as a list and one figure: the owners, and the number of them that must sign, such as "2 out of 3". Its inspection tool shows the owners, the threshold, the modules and the guards of an account in one view.

For Carbide Silica: the keys and the number of signatures as one line of text. A list is sufficient.

Recovery by guardians

Wallets with social recovery explain themselves by cases: a lost key, a stolen key, a guardian that does not answer. Each case has its outcome in words, and a waiting time in which the owner can stop a false recovery.

For Carbide Silica: the outcomes of a lost key and a stolen key, in words, with the names of this identity's authorities. All cases in view at one time.

Security models

Lampson's access control matrix is the standard form for "who can do what": a row for each party, a column for each action. Its known limit is that it is one moment and does not show change.

For Carbide Silica: a table of the powers and their holders for the version in view, and a second form for the change through time.

Vault designs

The Bitcoin vault proposals explain a delayed withdrawal as a sequence on a time line: the request, the delay, and the moment that funds can move. In the delay, a recovery key can pull the funds back. The delay is the protection, and the explanations show its length.

For Carbide Silica: say the delay of the lock in blocks and in time, and say what the authorities can do in that time.

Operations dashboards

Grafana's state timeline shows the state of each item as a horizontal band through time. The length of a bar is the time in that state, and equal states in sequence are one bar.

For Carbide Silica: one lane for each power. An authority that did not change in 21 versions is one bar, not 42 rows.

Personal histories

Plaisant and others made LifeLines for records of persons: facets as lanes, periods as lines, and events as marks, with the full history on one screen. Their aim was that a person does not miss a part of the record.

For Carbide Silica: the full life of the identity on one line of block heights, with a revocation as a mark that is hard to miss.

Real identities, drawn two ways

Select an identity, then a form. The first form is the control drawing and the version line of the page today. The second is the proposal.

The lanes have one step for each version, and the line above them shows the true block of each version. In the lanes, a change of colour is a change of holder. In the lock lane, a solid bar is a lock and a striped bar is an unlock that waits for its height. A red band is a time when the identity was revoked.

Recommendation

Replace the control drawing with a table of powers and two sentences of outcomes. Replace the version dots and the list of earlier authorities with lanes that have one step for each version.

  1. One sentence first. "One key controls Cape Town@. Medina@ can revoke it and Davos@ can recover it. The funds are locked: an unlock takes 2,880 blocks, about 2 days."
  2. A table of four powers. Spend and update, revoke, recover, and the lock. Each row has the holder as a link and one line that says what the power does.
  3. Two outcomes in words. "If the key is lost" and "If the key is stolen", made from the rows of the table. An identity that is its own authority gets the words "no other identity can stop or recover it".
  4. Lanes for the history. One lane each for the keys, the revoke authority, the recover authority and the lock. Each version is one step, because the updates of an identity frequently come in groups of some days with years between them. A time line above the lanes shows the true block of each version, as the version line does now. A bar is the versions for which one value was in effect, and a selection of a step shows that version.
  5. Revocation is a band. The versions in which the identity was revoked have a red band across all lanes.
  6. The lock has three states. Locked with a delay, unlock requested with the height at which it takes effect, and no lock.
  7. One step into the authorities. For each authority that is a different identity, one line says if it shares a primary address with this identity. Carbide already finds shared addresses.
  8. The evidence list keeps what is not in the lanes. The registration, the funding candidates, the referral chain and the import provenance stay. The rows of unchanged earlier authorities go, because the lanes show them.

What stays as Carbide gives it

  • The fields of each version, the status, and the steps between versions.
  • The evidence with its levels of confidence, and the funding candidates as candidates.
  • The referral chain and the payments of the registration.
  • The page for a tokenized identity, where a token holds the control.
  • The outcomes say what the protocol permits. They do not say who a person is or what a person will do.

Limits of this study

  • The 69 identities are those that received an output in 800 blocks. Identities that stake or trade are frequent in that set. The proportions for all identities can be different.
  • No identity in the sample has tokenized control, and one has more than one primary address.
  • The outcomes of the lock are from the description of Verus Vault. The study did not read the consensus code.
  • The first form on the page is a drawing by the rules of the app, not a screen capture.
  • The page does not test the forms with people.
